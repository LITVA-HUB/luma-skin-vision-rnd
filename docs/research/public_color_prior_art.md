# Public color constancy: primary-source review

Evidence cutoff: 2026-09-10. **LITERATURE REVIEW; NO REPRODUCED RESULTS.** No image or checkpoint was downloaded for this review. This starts a public benchmark track; the synthetic milestone `milestone/synthetic-only-2026-09-10` and earlier `prior_art.md` remain historical evidence, not public-data validation.

## Scope and important overlap

Target: compact single-image selective color constancy on real public linear camera RGB, with held-out camera/sensor evaluation, no target-camera calibration or target-batch adaptation, no cloud/foundation inference, and measured RTX 4060 8 GB costs. White-balance reliability is useful to downstream color processing; that motivation is **not new**. Confidence pooling, uncertainty estimation, combinations of illuminant hypotheses, residual correction, and cross-camera generalization all have substantial prior art.

## What the public ground truth actually supports

[Cube++ owner description](https://github.com/Visillect/CubePlusPlus) identifies 4,890 raw captures using Canon 550D/600D sensors of the same type. **SimpleCube++ is 2,234 four-times-downscaled linear images**, selected where left/right cube illuminant estimates differ by less than 1 degree, with one illuminant target per image. It is not a 2,234-camera benchmark. Its single-illuminant filtering suppresses some real ambiguity; selection success there may overestimate reliability under mixed light. Never count Cube++, SimpleCube++, their JPEGs or resized variants as independent data. Mask calibration objects before model input.

The neutral cube supplies illuminant chromaticity in camera RGB. It does not establish spectrophotometer-traceable CIELAB targets for scene surfaces, facial color, or a physical downstream Delta E 2000 benchmark. A generic ColorChecker catalogue Lab table also does not prove that the pictured physical chart has those measured values under the documented capture/viewing geometry.

For positive predicted illuminant `e` and reference illuminant `g`, use the established errors in degrees:

```
recovery(g,e) = acos(clip(dot(g,e)/(norm(g)*norm(e)), -1, 1)) * 180/pi
q = g / e                    # element-wise; reject invalid/zero predictions
reproduction(g,e) = acos(clip(sum(q)/(sqrt(3)*norm(q)), -1, 1)) * 180/pi
```

These are scale invariant. The reproduction definition is from [Finlayson & Zakizadeh, BMVC 2014, equations 1 and 17](https://www.bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/2014/files/paper047.pdf), with a later [journal account](https://research-portal.uea.ac.uk/en/publications/the-reproduction-angular-error-for-evaluating-the-performance-of-/). It concerns the residual cast of a neutral surface after diagonal correction; it is neither a new metric nor a measurement of all downstream surface colors. Epsilon clipping must be documented and must not hide invalid estimates.

### Are traceable public surfaces available elsewhere?

[Foster's natural-scene hyperspectral data](https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/david.foster/Hyperspectral_images_of_natural_scenes_04.html) offers a scientifically meaningful lead: effective reflectance spectra, grey reference measurements using a PR-650 with NPL-traceable calibration, and processing details. However, its terms explicitly restrict data to personal use. **Not cleared for this commercial R&D track.** Moreover, synthesized camera RGB from these spectra would be a spectral simulation experiment, distinct from real unseen-camera captures; effective reflectance includes geometry/interreflection effects.

[SFU's owner catalogue](https://www.cs.sfu.ca/~colour/data/) lists camera calibration, reflectance, controlled-light and image resources. The catalogue does not establish a permissively licensed, aligned real-camera/per-surface Lab dataset sufficient for this project. These are research leads, not adopted benchmarks. This bounded review found **no verified commercially permissible replacement** that simultaneously supplies real camera images, aligned instrument-measured surface references, defined D65/2-degree rendering and sufficient unseen-camera coverage.

An eventual physical Delta E 2000 experiment requires independent measured patch/surface reflectances or Lab references, alignment/masks, traceable illuminant/observer/geometry, and a fixed radiometric/camera-to-XYZ pipeline. Camera chromaticity alone cannot identify this mapping. Use the [Sharma, Wu & Dalal implementation notes and test data](https://hajim.rochester.edu/ece/sites/gsharma/ciede2000/) to verify the formula if such data becomes available. For now report angular selective risk and label any RGB/Delta E simulation explicitly as simulation.

# Modern visual training mechanisms for a compact photometric model

**LITERATURE/SOURCE EVIDENCE, accessed 2026-09-10.** This note covers contemporary visual representation training, including relevant 2024–2026 developments and the earlier DINOv2/SILC foundations. It supports a single-image model intended to generalize across scenes, surfaces, illumination and processing. It does not establish universal color recovery, new mathematics, measured accuracy, production clearance, or facial CIELAB/DeltaE performance. No models, datasets or training dependencies were downloaded; no experiment was run for this note.

The transferable opportunity is to train a small model with better information preservation and supervision. A semantic foundation encoder is not automatically a photometric estimator: robustness to a color change can mean that the encoder discarded exactly the illumination change that the estimator must measure. The proposed research should retain photometric evidence explicitly while using local structure and semantic context to interpret it. This is an **inference and research direction**, not a result reported by the cited labs.

## What the recipes actually teach

### DINOv2 and DINOv3: a moving teacher is only part of the mechanism

**Source facts.** DINOv2 combines image-level teacher/student matching, masked patch prediction, feature-distribution regularization, curated data and distillation. It is not simply a generic network trained to match itself. [DINOv2 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07193v2)

DINOv3 maintains both global and dense objectives. Its Gram-anchoring step preserves pairwise similarities of normalized patch features against a teacher snapshot; feature coordinates themselves may move. The paper reports that prolonged training can improve global representations while degrading locality. Its data recipe combines hierarchical balance, retrieval and high-quality data sampling, followed by resolution adaptation and distillation. [DINOv3 paper, Sections3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10104v1)

The model card documents the DINO/iBOT/KoLeo/Gram objectives, FSDP2 with reduced-precision matrix multiplication, and distillation from a frozen giant teacher. Its1,689M-image web corpus comes from a17B-image Instagram pool; the separate satellite corpus contains493M samples from Maxar imagery. These are not newly available permissive training sets for this project. [DINOv3 model card](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/dinov3/main/MODEL_CARD.md)

**Photometric inference.** A local-relation objective could keep neighboring surface evidence useful while the global estimator learns priors. However, a cosine/Gram target cannot guarantee retention of absolute channel scale, and feature-space rotations need not preserve physical meaning. Keep a separate measured RGB/chroma path. Compare a shallow intermediate-feature readout with the final pooled readout before adding a larger network. A teacher snapshot should be chosen on held-out task evidence, not because an earlier checkpoint has attractive false-color feature plots.

### SigLIP2 and SILC: separate objectives and stage their introduction

**Source facts.** SigLIP2 adds decoder-based localization/caption supervision, then EMA local-to-global and masked patch objectives late in training. The standard recipe adds these at80% completion, using one teacher global view, eight student local views and50% masking for the patch task. Smaller fixed-resolution variants also use teacher/student learnability scores for active data selection. The NaFlex branch deliberately omits self-distillation and masked prediction; do not attribute every component to every released variant. [SigLIP2 recipe](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14786v1)

The local-to-global mechanism is explicitly inherited from SILC, whose preprint dates to2023-10-20 and whose conference version is ECCV2024. SILC studies the gap between image-text alignment and useful dense visual features, adding EMA self-distillation as an auxiliary objective. [Original record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13355), [ECCV paper](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/03093.pdf)

**Photometric inference.** Auxiliary supervision can be added after the supervised illuminant estimate is useful, rather than allowing an early noisy teacher to define all targets. The teacher and student should receive coordinated transformations with an explicit target law. A cropped surface may lack information needed to infer the global illuminant, so forcing every small crop to mimic one precise global prediction can produce overconfidence. Match overlap-local evidence or a teacher distribution with uncertainty, and preserve the supervised anchor.

### V-JEPA2 and2.1: prediction targets determine which details survive

**Source facts.** V-JEPA2 learns masked latent prediction from a large image/video mixture; action-conditioned planning is a later stage using robot trajectories. The representation-learning and robot-control claims have different inputs and objectives. Its pretraining scale exceeds one million video hours. [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1), [Meta announcement](https://ai.meta.com/blog/v-jepa-2-world-model-benchmarks/)

V-JEPA2.1 extends prediction to **visible and masked tokens**, applies supervision at multiple encoder depths, and uses separate image/video tokenizers. Its analysis connects visible-token supervision to preserving local spatial structure. It expands image diversity and changes sampling of video sources; a model trained mostly on static images should not inherit a video curriculum unchanged. [V-JEPA2.1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14482v3)

**Photometric inference.** Train a shallow photometric representation to retain useful evidence at visible locations as well as infer missing evidence. Masking alone can reward reliance on context while neglecting faithful visible measurements. A context predictor should predict a distribution over missing color evidence, not invent a unique true reflectance. Video correspondence can later provide useful supervision, but unknown auto-exposure, white balance, moving light, highlights and shadows break naive temporal color consistency. These restrictions are not resolved by a video model's action-recognition accuracy.

### Perception Encoder and VideoPrism: transfer relations, inspect the readout

**Source facts.** PE shows that its best embeddings for different tasks occur at different internal layers. Spatial alignment combines its own frozen intermediate features with correspondence information derived from SAM mask logits; the latter is a relational teacher rather than direct copying of raw SAM features. The paper's visualizations also warn that a chosen low-dimensional projection can change how coherent features appear. [PE paper, Sections3–5 and AppendixB.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13181v1)

VideoPrism uses a heterogeneous captioned/noisy-text video corpus and global-local embedding distillation with token shuffling. This offers a useful example of obtaining complementary supervision from high-quality and weakly described data. It does not imply that captions specify illuminant, spectral reflectance or a colorimetric reference. [VideoPrism paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13217)

**Photometric inference.** If a teacher is eventually used, surface boundaries, patch correspondence or reliability may be safer targets than its final invariant semantic vector. A semantics-only teacher is unsuitable as the sole oracle for illumination. Teacher outputs are pseudo-labels with failure modes; disagreement should remain observable rather than being trained away. Multiple teachers are justified only by a measured gain over an equally expensive self-teacher or supervised control.

### Data curation: balance the intended domain, not merely web semantics

**Source facts.** Vo et al. propose successive hierarchical k-means followed by balanced sampling, with experiments on web images, satellite images and text. The first submission is2024-05-24. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15613)

**Photometric inference.** For this task, semantic diversity is insufficient. Sampling must also cover neutral-pixel scarcity, surface chroma, spatial color diversity, shadows, clipping, signal level, illuminant color, mixed light and known processing state. A common surface under multiple lights and multiple surfaces under one light are complementary evidence. A cluster balance that removes rare low-light or heavily colored scenes can improve average semantic scores while weakening the desired operating domain. Keep a uniform sampling floor and audit per-group retention when exploring adaptive sampling; do not rank samples using test residuals.

## Five highest-value lessons for the next experiment

The following are **proposed adaptations**, not published color-constancy results or novelty claims.

1. **Define invariance per output before choosing augmentations.** A geometry/context representation may be stable under a color intervention while an illuminant estimate must transform. For a declared positive diagonal change `x'=D x` in unclipped linear RGB, use `e'=normalize(D e)`. Compare corrected ratios `x/e` projectively or after a specified common normalization. Do not force `e(x')=e(x)`. Evaluate actual transformed error as well as consistency: two wrong predictions can be consistent.

2. **Preserve local photometric evidence throughout training.** Add a lightweight readout at one intermediate stage, or a local loss at visible and masked patches, while keeping raw linear chroma statistics available to the final estimator. Use validity masks to avoid supervising clipped/invalid channels. A decisive ablation is final-only versus intermediate supervision at fixed model capacity and update count; another replaces learned local targets with inexpensive measured patch statistics. Relation loss alone is not sufficient.

3. **Use a staged, supervised teacher rather than ungrounded self-agreement.** Warm up on real source GT, then initialize an EMA teacher from that model. Add a small-weight auxiliary term gradually, with teacher inference under no-grad. Retain the same supervised loss and full-population error reporting. Compare EMA against a frozen useful checkpoint and a no-teacher control. If the auxiliary term improves consistency but worsens GT error, preserve that failure and remove it.

4. **Make multiview supervision respect the information in each view.** Use geometric overlap for local correspondence. Under single uniform illumination, a crop shares the image illuminant but can be ambiguous; train its evidence weighting or uncertainty accordingly. Under mixed illumination, the crop need not share one global label. Avoid center-crop pipelines that discard the few useful neutral/specular regions. Prefer an explicitly bounded aspect-preserving resize/pad policy and measure its effect.

5. **Curate for identifiable, diverse photometric conditions and evaluate separately.** Use train-only condition strata, a uniform floor and capped hard-example weights. Give low-confidence examples their own diagnostics rather than deleting them as unlearnable. After any source benefit, lock the recipe and test on held-out scene/surface/illumination/processing groups; camera is one axis among them. Distill into a smaller deployed model only after the teacher's real task benefit exists.

These lessons are causally linked: a local objective is useful only if its transformation law preserves task information, and a teacher is useful only if its targets improve measured error in the intended domain. More views, losses, parameters or data cannot be treated as the mechanism effect without matched controls.

## What must not transfer unchanged

The official DINOv2 augmentation code applies brightness/contrast/saturation/hue jitter, random grayscale, blur and solarization. This is concrete evidence of a semantic-training augmentation policy, not an assumption about all SSL systems. [Source implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/dinov2/main/dinov2/data/augmentations.py)

| Transformation or practice | Proposed task-specific treatment | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Hue/saturation jitter and grayscale | Do not use as label-preserving illuminant augmentation. If used for a separate context branch, prevent it from becoming the only path to the prediction. | Destroying or relabeling the target signal without recording it. |
| Common exposure multiplier | Normalized illuminant direction may remain unchanged only within the declared linear, unsaturated model. Preserve noise/clipping metadata. | Calling clipped or nonlinear exposure changes exact invariances. |
| Positive channel gains | Transform illuminant targets and masks under the declared linear law. Preserve both original and intervened images as one source-derived training unit. | Treating gains as semantic color jitter with unchanged GT. |
| Arbitrary3×3 mixing | Restrict to a physically justified, admissible model; separate the numerical identity from the operating domain. | Recreating a full invariant frame that discards useful priors or ignores processing nonlinearities. |
| Gamma, tone curves, local contrast, denoising, sharpening | Treat as explicit processing variables or bounded nuisances with domain tests; use reversible known transforms only where their assumptions hold. | Claiming unknown JPEG/rendering can be inverted to unique sensor-linear truth. |
| Strong blur or aggressive crop/masking | Limit and ablate; preserve enough evidence and track resulting ambiguity. | Removing neutral regions, high-frequency reflectance clues, highlights or mixed-light boundaries while retaining a precise label. |
| Global feature normalization and mean/std preprocessing | Audit which absolute/relative channel information remains accessible; keep measured photometric inputs. | Confusing stable semantic embeddings with calibrated color measurements. |
| Teacher-generated captions, semantic masks or feature PCA | Treat as context/pseudo-supervision with measured failures. | Calling an object label, smooth mask or attractive visualization reflectance/illuminant GT. |

Even perfect recovery of a3-vector illuminant is not universal surface-color recovery. A single processed image can be compatible with different reflectance/illumination/processing combinations. The model should state its operating domain and uncertainty rather than manufacture an unknown original color. This is an identifiability restriction, not a shortcoming that frontier model size alone removes.

## Affordable RTX4060 route

The following is **PLANNED**, with no measured speed, VRAM fit or improvement claimed. The final architecture belongs to the broader synthesis.

**First screen: task-trained self-teacher, no foundation dependency.** Use a2–5M student with the same-sized EMA teacher, real source GT and a protected photometric path. Begin with one supervised view; then add one coordinated transformed/masked view and a low-dimensional local auxiliary head. Start at96–160px with modest token counts, and evaluate a224px final stage only if real source validation improves. Gradient accumulation can preserve effective batch size without multiplying concurrent activation memory. Keep teacher forwards under no-grad; serial view execution can lower peak activation storage.

As a memory accounting illustration,5M parameters at FP32 require20MB per full parameter copy. Weights+gradients+two Adam moments+one EMA copy total approximately100MB before activations, temporary tensors, mixed-precision copies, framework workspaces and data buffers. Thus parameter count alone is not a VRAM-fit claim; measure peak allocation on representative resolutions and crops. No256-GPU/2,048-TPU recipe is being reproduced.

**Teacher alternative only after a useful task baseline exists.** Train a somewhat larger own teacher on the same authorized source population, or investigate one exact licensed small frozen encoder as a training-only source of local context. Distill a narrow target—surface relationships, evidence reliability or intermediate context—not an entire semantic vector into the illuminant output. Cache only source-train teacher outputs when valid, and distinguish cached-view limitations from online transformed views. The deployed model remains one small single-image network.

**Decisive matched experiments.** Hold data, student capacity and optimizer-update budget fixed for: supervised-only; supervised+EMA output consistency; supervised+local visible/masked evidence; supervised+intermediate relation preservation; and the same schedule with naive color-invariant distillation as a declared negative control. Count extra teacher passes and wall time, because update matching alone does not match compute. Add data-curation changes only after one training-mechanism comparison is interpretable.

Record ordinary source error, tail/reproduction errors, transformed-pair error, invalid rates and per-condition results. Retain a held-out source validation split for architecture choice and a separate residual-calibration split for acceptance thresholds. Crops and transformations of one source image remain in its original split. Final evidence requires genuinely held-out scenes/surfaces/lights/processing conditions; augmented source images are not an independent benchmark. Report the inherited public-data limitations rather than asserting a broad domain from a narrow source set.

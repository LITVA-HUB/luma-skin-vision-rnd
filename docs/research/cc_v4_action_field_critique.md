# Critique of a continuous correction-action risk field

Research-only assessment, 2026-09-10. No implementation, model/data download, GPU use, or new empirical result. Equations below are direct derivations; proposed mechanisms and predicted benefits are hypotheses. The scope is global white balance with a positive illuminant reference, not restoration of arbitrary unknown surface colors.

## Verdict

An action-conditioned risk field is a defensible research candidate, but its basic formulation is a different parameterization of conditional decision risk. It does not create additional supervision information from a single illuminant label. The decisive control is a compact illuminant posterior evaluated through the exact reproduction-risk formula, with the same encoder, teacher, augmentations, parameter budget and selection procedure. Beating an ordinary point predictor alone would not establish the value of a learned action field.

The narrowest credible hypothesis is: **explicitly transporting cached local photometric evidence under candidate corrections, and fitting the resulting conditional risk with value-and-derivative supervision, can improve decision quality per unit of inference cost compared with a matched posterior decoder.** This is an efficiency/inductive-bias hypothesis, not new information, a new energy-based learning principle, or a new physical identifiability result. It remains untested.

## Exact target and its strongest null

Fix the action convention before any experiment. Let an action be the log chromaticity of the estimated illuminant **divisor**:

\[
a=(a_R,a_B),\quad \hat e(a)=(\exp a_R,1,\exp a_B).
\]

For positive true illuminant \(e\), put \(b=(\log(e_R/e_G),\log(e_B/e_G))\). The reproduction residual, up to irrelevant scale, is

\[
r(b,a)=(\exp(b_R-a_R),1,\exp(b_B-a_B)),\quad
S=\sum_i r_i,\quad Q=\sum_i r_i^2.
\]

The squared-sine reproduction risk and its action derivatives are exactly

\[
c(b,a)=1-\frac{S^2}{3Q},\qquad
\frac{\partial c}{\partial a_j}
=\frac{2Sr_j}{3Q}\left(1-\frac{Sr_j}{Q}\right),\quad j\in\{R,B\}.
\]

If action instead means a multiplicative correction gain, replace \(a\) by \(-a\); the gradient and joint transformation signs change. Positive RGB implies \(0\le c<2/3\) for finite actions, with supremum \(2/3\) at an extreme channel ratio. The corresponding angle is below \(\arccos(1/\sqrt3)\approx54.7356^\circ\). This is a narrower range than the generic sine-squared range. The risk is zero exactly at \(a=b\).

There are two essential analytic nulls:

1. **Point null:** \(R_{point}(x,a)=c(\hat b(x),a)\). Its unconstrained minimum is exactly \(\hat b(x)\); searching this field cannot improve that point prediction. It is useful for checking search machinery and accounting for extra computation.
2. **Posterior null:** \(R_{post}(x,a)=\int c(b,a)p_\theta(b\mid x)\,db\). For a finite weighted posterior this is simply \(\sum_k w_k(x)c(b_k(x),a)\), with the analytic gradient obtained by the same weighted sum. It supports ambiguity and decision-aware correction without a learned action network. Compare both fixed-support and small learned-mixture variants if their costs are practical; a deliberately poor unimodal null would be insufficient.

A particularly cheap strong control predicts nonnegative normalized weights over source-defined log-chroma support. Values and gradients are analytic; a fixed candidate grid permits a precomputed cost matrix. Relative support around a predicted anchor is another option, but its transform law depends on that anchor. These are ordinary posterior/basis approximations, not a novelty proposal.

With squared value regression, the population optimum of an unrestricted learned field is \(E[c(b,a)\mid x,a]\). If the action sampling distribution is conditionally independent of the label given \(x\), this is the desired \(E[c(b,a)\mid x]\). If training samples actions around the ground-truth \(b\), then action itself leaks label information through the sampling scheme: the regression target becomes conditioned on that scheme. This is a material issue, not merely an optimization detail. Use an action design independent of GT given the input, or explicit importance correction to a stated reference action distribution with support coverage. Sampling around a detached model proposal is compatible with that conditional-independence requirement; sampling around GT is not automatically compatible.

For a correct posterior, \(R_{post}\) is exactly the Bayes risk for this cost. A different learned field cannot systematically outperform its optimal action under the same information and true objective. Practical gains can arise from finite capacity, posterior misspecification, estimation error, or a better training objective. These should be the claimed explanation.

## Redundant labels and identifiability

All action values and gradients for one labeled image are generated by the same two numbers \(b\). Increasing the number of sampled actions increases numerical constraints and optimization work; it does not increase the number of independent illuminant observations, scenes, surfaces, or lighting conditions. Confidence intervals and effective sample counts must remain grouped by original scene/image, including transformed variants.

Derivative supervision is established Sobolev training: Czarnecki et al. explicitly train on target values and available target derivatives. Its initial arXiv date is 2017-06-15 and the conference paper is NeurIPS 2017. A possible benefit here is improved approximation of decision geometry with fewer sampled actions, not a newly invented supervision class. [Primary paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/758a06618c69880a6cee5314ee42d52f-Abstract.html), [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04859); accessed 2026-09-10.

Use the gradient of the learned scalar field itself for derivative matching. An independently predicted vector head can fit derivative labels while disagreeing with the value field, producing a nonconservative search direction. A second angular-value head may help the actual reporting objective, but must have a clearly defined statistical target: \(E[\alpha\mid x,a]\neq\arcsin\sqrt{E[\sin^2\alpha\mid x,a]}\) in general. Minimizing expected squared-sine risk need not minimize expected angle; monotonicity only guarantees identical ordering for deterministic outcomes. Predeclare which head selects actions and report its actual objective.

An image-conditioned field still cannot identify an unknown illuminant when different reflectance/illumination combinations produce the same image. With a single global illuminant GT, it also cannot learn which local surface rendering is perceptually preferable under mixed illumination: its risk labels do not contain that judgment. Processing robustness beyond the declared physical transform family requires additional evidence and evaluation, not a universal interpretation of this target.

## Joint-action consistency has a precise, restricted domain

For a positive diagonal channel transform \(D\), let \(d\) be its two log channel ratios. Under the ideal linear, unclipped model,

\[
x'=Dx,\quad b'=b+d,\quad a'=a+d,
\qquad c(b',a')=c(b,a).
\]

The proposed consistency is therefore \(E(Dx,a+d)=E(x,a)\). This is a valid augmentation law only when the image, label and action obey that joint model and the representation retains the needed information. It does not extend unchanged to nonlinear tone curves, clipping, gamut mapping, local editing, mixed-light fields, or arbitrary 3-by-3 mixing.

There is a structural trap: if a cached representation is completely invariant to \(D\), but the action is supplied in absolute coordinates, exact consistency would require the same field to equal arbitrary translations of itself. A nonconstant useful field cannot do that over unrestricted translations. Either preserve a photometric anchor that transforms with \(D\), or use an explicitly transported action-relative representation. Semantic invariance may coexist in another feature stream; it must not erase the anchor.

Cached patch log chromaticities shifted by \(-a\), transformed histogram coordinates, and color-sensitive local evidence pooling offer a concrete mechanism. Action-dependent reweighting of these tokens can change which surfaces support a candidate without rerunning an image encoder. This creates no new information, but supplies a testable representation bias. Unknown clipping or processing must have validity/uncertainty features rather than be absorbed into a false exact-equivariance claim.

## Search, supervision distribution and calibration risks

- **Optimizer exploitation:** minimizing a learned field preferentially selects its negative approximation errors. Small average value/gradient error on random actions is insufficient. Measure error and true regret specifically at selected actions, plus a fixed diagnostic grid. Constrain the search domain and budget using source data, keep the best proposal as a candidate, and count all action evaluations in latency.
- **Training/search mismatch:** extreme actions have saturated risk and may dominate a uniform wide-range sampler while providing weak gradients. A mixture of fixed reference actions and detached proposal-local actions is plausible. Candidate generation and any hard-negative mining must be confined to training data and preserve the intended conditional-risk target.
- **Apparent extra data:** many actions per image can make training curves or uncertainty estimates look better without additional scene diversity. Teacher views, image augmentations, and action labels need the same original-scene grouping.
- **Calibration after selection:** hold out independent source calibration scenes, freeze the encoder, action domain, candidate count, optimizer, fallback policy and selecting head first, then calibrate the error of the actually chosen action. Calibration on arbitrary actions does not establish calibration after minimization. Changing the policy requires recalibration; source calibration does not guarantee coverage under unseen processing or illumination shifts.
- **Oracle decomposition:** on the same candidate set, report true best-candidate error and selected-candidate error separately. Their gap diagnoses ranking failure; the best-candidate error diagnoses proposal coverage. Continuous methods need a declared common diagnostic candidate set in addition to their deployed search results.

## Novelty boundary and decisive comparison

Candidate-corrected-image evaluation predates this proposal. Hernandez-Juarez et al., CVPR 2020, evaluate candidate illuminant corrections through a shared network and derive an estimate from an achromatic-likelihood posterior. The proposed cached continuous field differs in parameterization and computation, not in the general idea of evaluating corrections. [CVPR 2020 primary page](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Hernandez-Juarez_A_Multi-Hypothesis_Approach_to_Color_Constancy_CVPR_2020_paper.html), [arXiv, first submitted 2020-02-28](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12896); accessed 2026-09-10.

Li et al., CVPR 2026, iteratively white-balance an image, render pseudo-sRGB, obtain a residual-cast judgment from a LoRA-tuned VLM, and update the correction. Thus iterative correction or residual-cast evaluation is not a credible stand-alone novelty claim either. This comparison does not propose using its VLM or weights. [CVPR 2026 primary page](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Li_White-Balance_First_Adjust_Later_Cross-Camera_Color_Constancy_via_Vision-Language_Evaluation_CVPR_2026_paper.html), [arXiv, first submitted 2026-05-19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19613); accessed 2026-09-10.

The minimum experiment should compare C+ with identical teacher/augmentations, its point analytic field, a matched posterior analytic field, a direct neural action field, and the explicit local-evidence transport field. Value-only versus value-plus-gradient, fixed versus action-dependent local pooling, and teacher-off versus teacher-on isolate the proposed mechanism. Match trainable capacity as closely as feasible and separately match total training/inference cost; exact equality on every axis is impossible, so report the actual differences.

Retain independent held-out scene/surface/illumination/processing evaluation and the existing failed V3 controls. Select configurations on source validation only. A credible win needs actual reproduction improvement and tail/selective-risk behavior beyond C+ and the analytic posterior null; synthetic action-consistency or good field fit alone is not enough. If the posterior null matches or wins, prefer it: it offers coherent bounded values, exact derivatives, cheaper evaluation and a clearer uncertainty interpretation. A novel-sounding head is not a reason to retain extra computation.
